# Starter Flows — Ready-to-Use Examples

These are complete, real-world examples you can run with your assistant right now. Copy the prompts, fill in the blanks in [brackets], and paste them into Claude Code.

Each flow walks through a full task from start to finish.

---

## Flow 1 — Set Up Your Email Automation

**What this does:** Gets your assistant reading your inbox and drafting replies so you spend less time on email.

**Time:** 10–15 minutes to set up, saves hours every week.

**Before you start:** Make sure Google Workspace is connected. If not, see [GOOGLE-WORKSPACE-SETUP.md](GOOGLE-WORKSPACE-SETUP.md).

---

### Step 1 — Check what's in your inbox

Paste this prompt into Claude Code:

```
Read my last 10 emails and give me a summary. Tell me:
- Who sent them
- What they're about
- Which ones need a reply
- Which ones can be ignored
```

Your assistant reads your Gmail and gives you a clear list.

---

### Step 2 — Draft replies to the important ones

Once you see the summary, say:

```
Draft a reply to [the email from NAME about TOPIC].
Keep it under 100 words, friendly but professional.
End with a clear next step or question.
Don't send it yet — just show me the draft.
```

Review the draft. If you like it, say "Send it." If not, say "Make it [shorter / more casual / more direct]."

---

### Step 3 — Build a weekly email habit

Once you're comfortable, set up a standing instruction:

```
Every time I start a new session, automatically read my inbox and tell me:
1. Any emails that came in since we last spoke
2. Which ones need my attention today
3. Any deadlines or meetings mentioned

Remember this as my standard email briefing.
```

Your assistant will do this check automatically at the start of every session.

---

### What to try next

- *"Write a follow-up sequence for leads who haven't replied in 3 days"*
- *"Set up a template for [type of email you send often]"*
- *"Sort my inbox and tell me which senders I should unsubscribe from"*

## Flow 2 — Connect Your Tools for Agency Work

**What this does:** Connects your assistant to your key business tools so it can manage clients, content, and communications all in one place.

**Best for:** Agencies, consultants, and freelancers who work across multiple clients.

**Time:** 20–30 minutes to set up.

---

### Step 1 — Tell your assistant about your agency

Start with this prompt to give your assistant proper context:

```
I run a [type of agency — e.g. social media / marketing / web design] agency.
My main clients are [describe your typical clients].
I spend most of my time on [describe your 3 biggest time drains].
I want you to help me with [what you most want to automate].

Please update your memory with this information and confirm what you've saved.
```

---

### Step 2 — Set up a client brief template

```
Create a client brief template for my agency. It should capture:
- Client name and business type
- Their goal for this project
- Their target audience
- Their tone of voice (formal / casual / fun / professional)
- Key messages they want to get across
- What success looks like for them
- Deadline

Format it as a simple document I can fill in at the start of every new client project.
```

Save the template by telling your assistant: *"Save this as my standard client brief template."*

---

### Step 3 — Generate a week of content for a client

Fill in your client details and paste this:

```
I have a client who [describe what they do].
Their target audience is [who their customers are].
Their tone is [formal / casual / friendly / professional].
Their goal is [what they want — more leads / brand awareness / sales].

Create a week of social media content for them:
- 3 LinkedIn posts (under 200 words each)
- 2 Instagram captions with hashtag suggestions
- 1 short email newsletter intro (under 100 words)

Make it sound like a real person wrote it, not AI.
```

---

### Step 4 — Build a competitor report for a new client

```
My new client is [name or describe the business].
Research their top 3 competitors.
For each competitor, tell me:
- What they offer and how they position themselves
- What customers complain about them (check reviews if possible)
- Where my client could stand out and do it better

Format it as a report I can send to my client.
```

---

### What to try next

- *"Write a client proposal for [project type]"*
- *"Create an onboarding checklist for new clients"*
- *"Analyse [client's website] and tell me what they should improve"*
- *"Draft a price increase email to send to existing clients"*

## Flow 3 — Daily Reporting Flow

**What this does:** Gets your assistant to pull together a daily or weekly business report so you always know what's happening without digging through tools.

**Best for:** Business owners who want a regular check-in on their business without spending time on admin.

**Time:** 5 minutes to set up, then it runs automatically every session.

---

### Step 1 — Define what matters to you

Start by telling your assistant what you care about:

```
I want a daily business check-in every time I start a session.
Here's what I want to know:

1. Emails — any new leads, urgent replies, or important messages
2. Calendar — what's on my schedule today
3. Tasks — remind me of anything I said needed to be done
4. One priority — based on all of the above, what should I focus on first?

Please remember this as my daily briefing format.
```

---

### Step 2 — Add your business metrics (optional)

If you track numbers, add this:

```
I also want to track these numbers in my daily briefing:
[Choose from the list below or describe your own]

- New leads this week
- Revenue this month vs last month
- Outstanding invoices
- Social media posts published this week
- Proposals sent vs accepted

I'll give you these numbers manually for now. In the future, we can automate pulling them.

Please add this to my daily briefing template.
```

---

### Step 3 — Run your first full briefing

Ask for it now:

```
Run my full daily briefing.
Check my emails, calendar, and any tasks I've mentioned.
Then give me my one priority for today.
```

---

### Step 4 — Set up a weekly summary

For a bigger-picture view, ask once a week:

```
Give me a weekly business summary.
Look back at our conversations this week and tell me:
1. What did we accomplish?
2. What is still outstanding?
3. What should I focus on next week?
4. One thing I should consider doing that I haven't thought of yet.
```

---

### What to try next

- *"Create a weekly KPI report template for my business"*
- *"Help me build a simple system for tracking leads without a CRM"*
- *"Every Friday, remind me to review my week and plan next week"*
- *"Draft a monthly report I can send to my business partner / investor / team"*

## Tips for Getting the Most From These Flows

**Start simple.** Run each step once before adding more complexity. Make sure the basics work first.

**Teach your preferences.** If the output isn't quite right, say *"Do this again but [change]."* Then say *"Remember this preference for next time."*

**Chain tasks together.** Once you're comfortable, combine flows. For example: *"Run my daily briefing, then draft replies to the emails that need them."*

**Save what works.** Tell your assistant: *"Save this as a saved flow I can reuse."*

**Ask questions.** If you're not sure what your assistant can do, just ask: *"What would be the best way for you to help me with [problem]?"*
