# Worked example: map the competitive landscape for a Selr AI workshop offering

A reference end-to-end session for a non-technical owner using competitive-cartographer to find the white space for a new product. **You:** is the user, **Claude:** is the response.

The skill's value is the 6-step process: Define Space, Identify Players, Analyse Positioning, Find White Space, Recommend Positioning, Stress-Test. This transcript walks all six against a real Selr AI offering.

---

## You

map the competitive landscape for my in-person AI workshop offering. I charge $1,500 AUD for a full-day, in-person workshop where business owners install AI into their business. I want to know where I'm differentiated and where I look the same as everyone else.

## Claude

Working through this as a six-step competitive mapping.

### Step 1: Define Space

Your space is "in-person AI workshops for non-technical Australian business owners." Two key boundaries:

- **In-person, not online.** Excludes Coursera, Udemy, LinkedIn Learning, and the $99-$499 self-paced video courses.
- **Operator-grade, not academic.** Excludes university short courses and government-funded reskilling programs that don't end with a working AI system.

The buyer is a business owner with revenue between AUD 250k and AUD 5M, no in-house tech team, and a paid AI tool stack they don't fully use.

### Step 2: Identify Players

Direct competitors (in-person AU-based AI workshops at the $1k-$5k tier):

1. **Stone & Chalk Sydney** - 2-day "AI for SMBs" intensives, AUD 1,800. Government-backed.
2. **General Assembly Australia** - 1-day "AI Tools" workshops, AUD 850-1,200. International brand, generalist.
3. **AI for Australia (Tim Pollock)** - half-day Brisbane sessions, AUD 600. Marketing-tools focus.
4. **Lighthouse AI Workshops (Melbourne)** - 1-day, AUD 1,500. Productivity-tool focused (Copilot, Notion AI).
5. **Independent consultants doing private installs** - typical AUD 5k-15k bespoke engagements.

Adjacent players (compete for the same buyer's wallet but not workshop-shaped):

6. **Skool communities** (yours included) - AUD 99-499/month recurring.
7. **n8n/Make-led automation freelancers** - AUD 2k-10k project-based.
8. **The "I'll figure it out myself with ChatGPT" non-customer** - free, dominant default.

### Step 3: Analyse Positioning

Per-player positioning summary:

- **Stone & Chalk**: government-backed, broad theory + light demo, generic SaaS. High trust, low specificity.
- **General Assembly**: international curriculum, instructor-led, generalist tool walkthroughs. High brand, low operator depth.
- **AI for Australia**: marketing-tool focused, low price, half-day format. Approachable, narrow scope.
- **Lighthouse**: productivity-tool focused (Copilot/Notion AI), 1-day, similar price to you. Direct overlap on format.
- **Independent consultants**: bespoke, high-touch, 5-15k. High depth, high price, no workshop format.
- **Selr AI (you)**: operator-grade install, autonomous Claude Code agents, AUD 1,500 same-day install of a working system the owner runs after you leave. Distinct deliverable.

### Step 4: Find White Space

Two clear gaps where nobody else is positioned:

**Gap 1: "Same-day working install" position.** Every direct competitor delivers training or theory. None delivers a working AI system the owner runs after the day ends. Stone & Chalk and General Assembly stop at "you now understand the tools." Lighthouse and AI for Australia stop at "here is a Copilot demo." You hand over a Claude Code system, GHL automation, or n8n flow that the owner owns from day one.

**Gap 2: "Australian SMB operator who is not a developer" persona.** Stone & Chalk and General Assembly are skewed to corporate L&D buyers. Independent consultants are skewed to companies with in-house tech. The owner-operator with no in-house tech and AUD 500k-3M revenue is genuinely underserved at the in-person workshop tier.

### Step 5: Recommend Positioning

Recommended headline positioning, anchored on Gap 1:

> "The only AI workshop in Australia where you leave with a working AI system installed, owned, and running in your business by the end of the day."

Defensible because: (a) every competitor at this price point delivers training or theory, not a working system; (b) the Claude Code autonomous-agent shape is technically distinct (not just a Copilot or Notion AI lesson); (c) you have proof (5,000+ build sessions logged, time-stamped community evidence).

Sub-positioning anchored on Gap 2:

> "Built for the owner-operator who runs the business themselves. No internal IT team required."

### Step 6: Stress-Test

Where this positioning could fail:

- **If Lighthouse pivots to also offer same-day installs**, you lose half of Gap 1. Mitigation: lock in the Claude Code dependency in your marketing copy (Lighthouse uses no-code Copilot, not autonomous code). The Claude Code surface is your moat.
- **If Stone & Chalk launches an "implementation track"** alongside their training, they take Gap 1 too. Mitigation: ship workshops more frequently than they can react. You can run weekly; they run quarterly.
- **If buyers conflate "training" with "install"** in their head before they hear your pitch, they price-anchor on Stone & Chalk's AUD 1,800 and your AUD 1,500 looks just-a-bit-cheaper, not differently-shaped. Mitigation: lead landing-page copy with "you leave with a working AI system" as line 1, never with the workshop format.

White-space defensibility: high on Gap 1 (technical differentiation + proof), medium on Gap 2 (persona positioning is copyable by anyone willing to ignore enterprise L&D buyers).
